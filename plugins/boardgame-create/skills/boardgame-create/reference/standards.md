# Standards

The rules every game built with this skill must satisfy, distilled from three games and every correction the owner made along the way (`reference/corrections.md` has the full list with quotes). Each standard names its check: a script that fails, a judge that must report it, or evidence a reviewer must open. The gauntlet judges (`templates/judge-*.md`) are given this file and report each applicable standard as MET or NOT MET with evidence; a game does not ship with a NOT MET.

Owner's words are quoted where the standard came from one. "Check:" says how it is verified. Numbers are BUMBLE & BLOOM's shipped values unless stated; a new game may change a number with a reason written in `design.md`, never silently.

## A. The game

- **A1. The bar.** Fun for 2–4 adults, 20–40 minutes, one real innovation, cute and beautiful, a signature moment people retell. Check: `templates/judge-gameplay.md` against `reference/principles.md`'s table (verb, shared picture, stake in others' turns, private hope, clock, growth, talk, input randomness, time box, signature moment), every row filled in `design.md`.
- **A2. Designed from principles, then balanced.** The design document fills the principles table before any geometry; balance is measured, not asserted: self-play at every player count with seat-win rates within a few points of even, 7–9 rounds at four players (or the game's stated length), goals met 20–70 %, no runaway economy. Check: `node engine/bin/bg.js balance` and `bg stats` numbers in `iterations.md`; `verify_events.js` 0 illegal moves in the showcase and 60 games.
- **A2b. Every rule must pay.** Before the gameplay gate, play a deliberately dumb strategy that ignores each optional or interactive rule (one bee shuttling, never following, never dancing, ignoring goals) against the full AI; if the dumb strategy wins more than its fair share, the rules it ignores do not earn their place and the design goes back (BUMBLE's playtest, 2026-09-17: "we fly one bee back and forth ... what's the point of bees 2 and 3?"; the sim then showed the one-bee shuttle winning 66 % at two players against the tuned AI, and the owner's verdict: "the gameplay seems pretty lame and the rules are way too complex to justify that"). Check: `bg balance --gates` reports each ignored rule's cost in win rate; a rule that costs nothing to ignore is cut or made to matter.
- **A2c. Several live paths, no runaway, no stalemate.** The game has two or three named archetype strategies that each win between half and one and a half times their fair share at a mixed table, and each has an answer head to head; the midpoint leader wins fewer than three games in four at four players; the last round changes the winner in a measurable share of games; no seed reaches the turn guard. A mechanic the winner uses every turn while ignoring the rest is too strong or its alternatives too weak, and the fix is the number the ledger points to, never a buff into an auto-win (`reference/principles.md`, "Degenerate strategies"). Check: `bg balance --gates` (the archetype table, the midpoint-leader and last-round numbers) in `iterations.md`; the breaker agent's best line in the dumb-strategy table.
- **A3. No seat compensation.** Turn-order bias is fixed structurally (opening order, starting positions, rotating first player), never with bonus points for later seats. Check: the engine has no seat bonus; balance numbers per seat.
- **A4. Randomness at the input.** Chance enters through setup, draws or dice a player rolls, never through hidden state the wood could betray ("the players would easily learn to recognize the unique grain pattern on the back of each token"). Secret information lives on cards or in a bag, and the design says where randomness is. Check: judge-gameplay; the components list.
- **A5. Bought components are welcome.** Dice, marbles, cubes, glass beads, meeples and cards are ordinary components; the laser makes the parts and the box, and the box must hold what is bought (`reference/components.md` has sizes and holders). Never bend a design to avoid a die. Check: the components list names the bought items with sizes; `bg pack` packs them.
- **A6. The assistant decides gameplay.** Balance questions are settled from self-play and reported after; the owner is never handed a menu of options ("you make the decisions on gameplay"). Gameplay does not block the cut once everything else is ready: a physical playtest decides. Check: workflow.
- **A7. Everyday words.** Piece names, rules and box text use words a family knows ("beehive", not "skep"); part ids in code may keep technical names. Check: the game's `page.js` `banned` list; judge-rules.

## B. Parts and fits

- **B1. Fits are design values; stock is measured.** Every slot, tab, notch and box dimension derives from two caliper readings per stock (thinnest and thickest sheet: slots for the thickest, tab depths for the thinnest) and a kerf per stock, entered on the page and in the `#hash`. There are no fit-override inputs, rung ladders or tuned fits ("if the kerf and thickness is right, everything should fit"). Check: `engine/src/stock.js` has no inputs beyond stock readings and kerfs; the page's stock panel shows only those.
- **B2. Kerf compensation lives in the file.** Machine compensation is OFF on every layer; outlines are offset out and holes in by kerf/2; parts on shared lines sit one kerf apart with one cut on the midline; engraving is unioned and shrunk by kerf/2; engraving that reaches an edge bleeds past it (kerf/2 on fronts, 0.25 mm + kerf/2 on backs). Check: `svg_lint.js`; `parts.json` `meta.kerf_comp: "file"`; the sheet-0 kerf coupons drawn compensated with the drawn-for kerf engraved on them.
- **B3. The kerf formula.** The page tells the owner: kerf = drawn-for kerf + (20 − square)/2 + (hole − 20)/2 from the sheet-0 coupons. Without the drawn-for term it tells them to enter 0 and breaks every press fit. Check: page_check reads the formula.
- **B4. Press fits, no micro-features.** A press fit may rely on friction if it does not loosen with use. Interference acts on long cut edges or on a leaf spring, never on a rib, bump or crush feature under about 1 mm ("the machine has inertia. it isn't magic"), and never across the ply thickness ("i don't want crush features that rely on thickness"). Check: `engine/src/stock.js` FIT table; fit reviewer.
- **B5. The standee standard.** A standee is a single silhouette on a tab (10 mm wide, depth from the thinnest reading, 30° lead-in ramps on the tab corners) pressed into a base whose slot is a fixed-fixed leaf spring: the slot's two long edges extended into single-kerf slits, spring thickness 1.5 mm (never thinner, only bigger), corner reliefs at the slot ends; the shipped sweep point is an 8 mm span and a 9.5 mm finished slot. Bases are keyed: a bridge in the slot meets a notch in the tab at a different position for each kind, so a piece fits only its own base, and no piece is a mirror image of another. Both faces engraved. Check: sheet 0's BASE and TAB samples; `bg fit` at both stock extremes; the parts list shows one base kind per piece kind.
- **B6. Cross-lapped structures.** Two halves halved from the top and from below, pressed into a + hole (bar width from the tab width) with leaf springs at the hole ends; a 0.8 mm band hidden behind the crossing piece so no seam shows in the art. The tabs are 16 mm wide (owner, 2026-09-18: "the tabs on the cross-slot standees need to be wider"; 10 before). Each slot runs at least 12 mm through its half's wood, measured along the slot's walls (a spring slit up the centre line does not count against it): from the silhouette's own edge to the halving line for the top slot, from the halving line down through the tab for the bottom one; shorter, the halves rack and the joint works loose (owner, 2026-09-18). Check: the engine's `crosslap` (`DESIGN.XLAP_MIN_SLOT`) refuses a shorter slot when the parts build; fitcheck assembly scenes; art judge.
- **B7. Handling.** Anything in a recess stands proud enough to pick up ("impossible to pick up with human hands"); no push holes; a thin top layer (1.5 mm) over a thicker backing is the standard tray. Tokens are single pieces; no per-piece glue; no multi-part tokens. Check: fit reviewer with the assembled scene; the components table lists thicknesses.
- **B8. Parts within ±0.07 mm of drawn; every clearance explicit.** Every clearance is drawn at finished size from the face actually met at the built thickness, and the checker places parts where they seat during assembly, not only at their drawn positions. Check: `fitcheck.js` scenes with the window open (`t3lo` thin, `t3hi` thick) and each single-ended stock; the mismatch case (files drawn for the range, stock at one end).
- **B9. The wood outranks the model.** When a cut part contradicts a check or a stress model, the part wins: measure it, correct the model or the standard, say so plainly. Sub-millimetre geometry is downsampled by the machine and its software. Check: `iterations.md` records every physical measurement and what changed because of it.
- **B10. Coupons, once.** Sheet 0 carries the kerf coupons (a 20 mm square and a 20 mm hole per stock) and one design-value sample of each joint; it is minimal, labelled with the drawn (uncompensated) sizes, and carries no instructional text. Production sheets carry no coupons. Test sheets are named, listed in the page's test section, and pruned when obsolete. Check: page_check's sheet catalogue; svg_lint counts.

## C. The box and its jig

- **C1. The shoulder box, from a library.** Two open trays drawn from one frame (`base_ease` 0.10 mm a side, `lid_ease` 0.05 mm a side: the lid's sliding fit over the neck grip is half the base's), finger joints at every corner with bands at both edges (no solid rim band), floor tabs on every edge (part of the outline, never separate rectangles), thumb notches to push the trays apart, a symmetric neck (two grip bands at each end, the middle band's fingers behind their mates, no orientation mark) making a 2.5–4 mm shadow line. `inner` is 60–400 mm a side or `[x, y]` a rectangle; `wall_h` 12–40 mm (24 is the default). It comes from `engine/src/box.js` and `engine/src/stock.js` and is not redesigned per game. Check: `bg fit` closed-box and packed scenes; `LID_EASE` is half `BASE_EASE`.
- **C2. Zero clearance plate to sides.** The floor plate spans the walls' inside faces at the thickest reading with no clearance ("no, zero clearance there"); the corner fingers are a press fit; the box is glued. Check: `engine/src/stock.js` (raised floor); fit reviewer.
- **C3. Everything fits inside, closed.** Every playing piece packs into the closed box the way a person would, verified piece by piece as 3D prisms, with the lid clearance reported. Check: `bg pack` FITS; the page's packed view.
- **C4. The glue jig is automatic and validated.** The 24 mm raised-floor shoulder box ships its glue jig (`bg jig`): one base with a two-bar torsion spring station per floor-tab gap (16 on a 190 mm box) and a ramped insert per station, on one sheet with any jig the game adds, drawn to the trays' mean size, pads 1 mm inside the drawn tray, at least 1 mm of stroke, fences short enough that a weight can sit on the tray. The cut jig "works perfectly as-is" (owner, 2026-09-17): its springs and interference are not to be redesigned. A short flush tray (`wall_h` under 24, `floor: 'flush'`) may not get this jig. Check: `bg jig` writes `jig.json` and `parts/jig-sheet.svg` when the box takes it.
- **C5. The outside of the box never sits on the honeycomb.** Every face that shows on the outside of the closed box (lid top, base underside, all eight wall faces) is engraved in the FRONT file, face up, because the face down on the honeycomb gets smoke-stained. Check: `parts.json` sheet metadata; page_check's sheet descriptions.
- **C6. The lid carries the hero art and the safety warning; the rules go inside the lid; the setup map inside the base; a medallion and the product code under the base.** Check: art judge (box group); `art.md`'s warning sizes.

## D. The laser process and the sheets

- **D1. Stock and margins.** Nominal stock 300 × 450 mm (the owner's 305 × 457 sheets, conservatively: "one fiducial missed the sheet"); parts centred between the long edges and along the sheet; at least 3 mm from the raw long edges (2.5 mm only where a proven block has no other slack, with the reason written down); equal 10 mm clamp keep-outs along both short edges; 15 mm right triangles kept clear at the corners for the magnetic clamps. Check: `parts.json` `meta.long_edge` and `clamp_spots`; svg_lint.
- **D2. Registration.** Two tiny crosses (two 3 mm cuts each) in the same place on every sheet, cut on the backs file in red and drawn green on the front; no datum strip; the corner marks are the parts' bounding box in their own colour (orange `#ff8000`; yellow is the vector fill). Files are written landscape with the registration on the left. Check: page_check's sheet list; svg_lint layer colours.
- **D3. Order of work.** Backs file first (engrave, then the crosses), flip side over side, fronts file: engrave, score the engraving edges at low power, inner cuts, then outlines. Every engraving's compensated edge gets a score line except where a cut runs ("makes them much sharper and they really pop"). Check: the files' layer order and the page's order-of-work text; `meta.edge_scores`.
- **D4. Raster along the long axis.** Every text line and the cover composition run along the sheet's long axis; repeated engravings are grouped in few bands; no engraved pockets where a thinner stock can cut them (D10). Check: svg_lint's raster warnings are read and answered.
- **D5. Shared cuts, cut once.** Parts nested against each other share one cut line one kerf apart; a part cut out of another part's opening (tiles in a frame) shares the opening's line (`share`, `share_holes`, `cut_comp` in lasergeom's layout) and no edge is cut twice. Check: the sheet SVG has no duplicate path along a shared edge (`shared-cuts` group carries it once); svg_lint.
- **D6. Sheet budget and one-piece parts.** At most four production sheets; every part is one piece; finger notches and tabs are part of the outline. Check: manifest counts; svg_lint.
- **D7. Two tones.** The engraver makes engraved or bare; a second tone is a second wood. Walnut engraves pale and with poor contrast, so readable engraving goes on basswood and walnut is used for shape and colour. Check: art judge; the materials table in `design.md`.
- **D9. Vector fill only for a large thick border, never by itself** (owner, 2026-09-21: "concentric should only ever be used for large thick borders. it shouldn't be auto selected, it should default to off and be an option"). Everything engraves as raster unless the designer chooses lines for one element: `spec.vector_fill` turns the planner on for a game, or a part passes its own `concentric_fill` result as `vfill` and keeps that element out of its raster. The lines are one spiral a pitch (0.1 mm, 100 lines per centimetre) apart from the edge inward with a lead-in and lead-out, so the laser's ramp never lands on a fresh line. Check: `parts.json` `meta.vector_fill.elements` is 0 unless the game asked. What follows is the planner, for when it is on: A large outline rastered costs every row of its bounding box, but whatever is drawn inside it comes free, because the machine software groups rasters; its algorithm is unknown, so the estimate assumes the best grouping any software could find (row by row, spans merged when the gap is under two run-ins: a lower bound) and errs towards raster: the layout's `vector_fill` converts a connected engraved element to concentric lines (100 lines per centimetre, one family of lines from the element's outer edge inward, mitred, clipped by its holes, plus each hole's edge; yellow layer, no blue edge score) only when the saving is dramatic (even that raster, the edge score it would need included, at least twice the lines, and 10 s left after that weighting for the shape as a whole; owner: "generally prefer raster where the savings aren't dramatic", and "30s is too dramatic ... 200 little token pieces ... could save an hour"), best gain first, then pairs sharing rows, savings recomputed after each conversion; one decision per shape, every copy of it in every part and on every sheet, so duplicate geometry always matches ("any duplicate geometry should match engraving style"); a part flagged `raster: true` (or a `raster` region) in the spec is never converted, whatever the sheet would save. Check: `bg lasertime` prints the decisions and the estimated minutes against the machine's own estimate (the calibration); svg_lint accepts `#ffff00` strokes; the page's legend says how to run the yellow layer; `bg concentric` for a shape drawn by hand. (Owner, 2026-09-17 and 2026-09-19: `reference/manufacturing.md`.)
- **D10. Pieces and the trays that hold them** (owner, 2026-09-18) are the engine's `tray` kind, never hand-drawn. Every pocket is its piece's outline plus **0.30 mm** all round. The pocket depth lets the piece be picked up easily: it stands at least 1 mm proud of the pocket's rim, else the pocket gets a finger notch. With a thinner stock in the game the pockets are **cut out of the thin stock** and, as material allows, laminated on a back of thin or thick stock; with no thin stock the pockets are **engraved** into the tray. `META.trays` tells the page where each pocket is, and the pieces sit in them on the table. **Storing pieces in their trays is the preference, not a rule**: by default the packer loads every pocket with its piece (or any piece of the same kind and outline) and stacks trays of one outline in one pile, each resting on the frame or on the pieces standing proud of the tray below; when that does not fit the box (a tray with 3 mm pieces is 6 mm a layer, and the pieces flat take far more floor than they do in piles: BUMBLE's 96 nectar tokens pack as eight piles in the gaps and "it all fits in the box", owner 2026-09-18), `store: false` on the tray keeps it empty in the box and the pieces pile as usual. Never grow the box or cut pieces to make the storage rule work. Check: `bg parts` refuses pockets nearer than 2 mm to each other or the edge; `bg pack` FITS either way; the parts viewer's tray assembly; fitcheck with the pieces in their pockets.
- **D8. No laser minimums enforced.** Strokes near 0.45 mm and gaps near 0.6 mm are legibility guidance and lint warnings; nothing fattens art or fails a build on those numbers alone. Check: svg_lint reports them as W, never E.

## E. Art

- **E1. Two tones, whole shapes.** Solid fills; detail inside a solid is a bare hole; no hatching, greys, opacities or unfilled strokes; whole honeycomb cells and whole leaves only; antennae 2 mm; stripes as curved bands clipped to the body. Check: art judge with `templates/judge-art.md`.
- **E2. Faces on plain wood.** No patch, spot or stripe over an eye or nose; both eyes, nose, mouth visible; every piece reads as what it is from 60 cm (silhouette test); distinct pieces are distinct by silhouette alone ("the mason and bumble bees might be a bit too similar in shape"). Check: art judge.
- **E3. One design language.** Icons and symbols are adopted as a set (all of one designer's, chosen by a blind A/B), never cherry-picked per glyph; card backs carry art; no mirror-image pieces. Check: art judge's blind A/B.
- **E4. Every figure from the cut files.** Nothing on the page or in the manual is hand-drawn; figures are rendered from `parts.json` at modest size (120–260 px on the page). Check: page_check embeds the SVGs; `manual/assets.js` regenerates figures.
- **E5. Lettering set, never cleaned.** Outlined lettering, one weight per face, cap height ≥ 2.2 mm for rules and ≥ 3 mm for labels, upright and consistently placed on every part; a lighter weight for the lid-inside rules. Check: `node engine/bin/bg.js lettering` 0 vanished or split glyphs.
- **E6. Judged fresh until NO FLAWS.** Each art group goes through a fresh independent judge every round until `NO FLAWS` and a blind A/B win against a named reference; builders never grade their own art. Check: `iterations.md` round log.

## F. Text on the wood

- **F1. A word earns its place** only if a player reads it during play at that spot and could not infer it: the title once, labels and values, one rules reference inside the lid, the legally required warning. Never handling instructions, rules digests or phase strips on playing surfaces, captions under icons that already read, slogans, or marks that carry no information (a symmetric part gets no "this side down"). Check: art judge; `build.js` banned-word scan.
- **F2. Plain sentences.** Every rule on wood, card or page is a complete plain sentence in play order; if a panel is too small, cut rules from the panel, never compress them into fragments. Check: judge-rules; judge-page.
- **F3. Test sheets carry no instructional text**, only drawn dimensions. Check: page_check's test-sheet cards.

## G. The page

- **G1. One self-contained file.** Everything embedded (part SVGs, sheets as downloads, the engine, the renderer, fonts, the rulebook pages); no external resources; loads offline; no console errors. Check: `engine/checks/page_check.js` offline group.
- **G2. The stage is the page.** No header, nothing scrolls. A full-viewport 3D stage: two closed boxes, the title over them, and one button, "Open the box". The button runs the opening in time (BUMBLE: 10 s; the owner found 20 s "too slow. maybe double the speed"): the far box dissolves, the lights come up, the box lies down and opens, every piece flies to its table place (the pieces get most of the time, from p 0.40; the owner: "the box opening animation spends too much time on the rear-facing box fade-out and zoom, and too little time spreading the pieces out"), then the AI game starts by itself with a transparent log and no controls, from the opening's own last camera (the camera's last keyframes are the table's home framing, so there is no zoom step when the game starts: "there's a little jump when the gameplay starts - zoom changes. get rid of that"). The same button reads "Close the box" while the box opens or the game runs; pressing it packs the live game back into the box (the opening runs backwards from the live table), and opening it again lets the game go on. Everything is a pure function of one progress value. (Scroll-driven until 2026-09-18; the owner: "let's get rid of the scrolling mechanism and just have a button to open the box and a button to close the box. scroll can go back to being zoom when the box is open.") Check: page_check's opening and flow groups; `engine/checks/opening_check.js` (no piece passes through another; pieces leave the box top first); `engine/checks/scroll_sheet.js` contact sheets of the button opening on a real GPU.
- **G3. Modals, not sections.** The rulebook modal is the book: no heading, no toolbar, no page selector, no help line (owner, 2026-09-18: "get rid of the controls and text on the rulebook modal. it should just be click or swipe to turn the page. make the page turn animation nicer, with more page bend"). Compact PDF links sit under the book, only there. Each modal has a GET string so a reload returns to that view (`?rules=4`, `?parts=<id>`, `?files=<sheet>`). A click on a page turns it (the right page forward, the left back), a swipe drags it, the arrow keys work, and the turning leaf bends (hinged strips) with a shadow on the table under it, never a drop shadow drawn in the leaf's own plane. On a desktop viewport the open book is a two-page spread of the trimmed page, not the print sheet. The rulebook (the real print rulebook as a page-turning book), the parts viewer (assemblies with Explode, the jig assembly, the packed box) and the laser files (production sheets in rows: name, front, back, description; test sheets as a card grid; the colour legend; the order of work; the stock inputs with the kerf formula) open over the stage. Nothing is duplicated: the rules appear once (the book), the rulebook files are linked in one place. Check: page_check's modal groups.
- **G4. Nothing nobody reads.** No lede paragraph, no marketing copy, no fit-tolerance boilerplate, no stale sections ("no one will read this, not even me"); the self-play numbers, if shown, are labelled as balance checks. Check: judge-page against this list.
- **G5. Live stock inputs.** Thickness readings and kerfs typed on the page regenerate the parts, the 3D view and the downloads in the page (a worker), with the same code that runs under node. Check: page_check's regeneration group.
- **G6. Free orbit, wheel zooms.** Drag to orbit, right-drag to pan, the wheel (and a pinch) zooms: on the closed boxes, through the opening, and once the game is running. A modal over the stage still eats the pointer. Check: page_check.
- **G7. Fast first frame, a loading screen until it.** The boxes are on screen as soon as the scripts have run: a `#loading` screen covers the stage from the first byte and is hidden by the first frame; any long work runs after it (BUMBLE's flight plan is a generator advanced 8 ms per frame, and the opening waits at the edge of the flights, with the loading screen back and see-through, until the plan is ready; `window.__intro.ready` tells the harnesses). The *look* of that screen (colour, type, animation, the word) is the game's `page.css`, not the engine's. Heavy data is in the page once and parses after page.js: each sheet SVG in one `<script type="text/plain">` block, never twice as base64 in an img and a link (that was 26 MB of the 33 MB page and most of its load time; owner, 2026-09-18: "the 3D loads a bit slow. can we load in the boxes first and then load the rest of the parts? and also we should probably have a loading animation until we're ready to display"). No per-frame readbacks. Check: page_check (`#loading`, blob download links, the ready hook); the reviewer loads it from file://.
- **G8. World-class or not done.** The owner's bar for the page is "an extremely cool, world-class" animated site; a page that works but does not delight is FIX. Check: judge-page opens the real page and presses the button.
- **G9. The page is this game's style.** Type, colour, the loading screen, the title, the chrome: the game's `page.css` and `fonts/` (named in `game.json.fonts`). The engine's page CSS is layout only and does not pick a face. A game that still looks like the starter has not finished. Check: `page.css` exists; `fonts/` holds the three weights; judge-page.

## H. The rulebook

- **H1. End-user only.** Nothing about cutting, production, kerf, glue, jigs or assembly in the rulebook; those live on the page's files modal and in the skill. Check: `manual/verify.js` and `check-references.js`; judge-rules.
- **H2. Structure, voice and design** as `reference/manual.md`: cover, what's in the box with what each piece does, setup with exact maps per player count, the turn, worked examples, end and scoring, reference (rulings, glossary, keep-open summary, index); plain sentences a patient friend would say; two reading paths; figures from the cut files; the small-parts warning. Check: `manual/verify.js` (every printed number from the engine), `check-references.js`, `review-layout.js` (no overflow, no orphans); judge-rules until no GUESS and no wrong answers.
- **H3. Commercial quality, judged.** Catan-grade: iterated with a fresh independent judge until it grades A; the cover and every figure match the current art. Check: `iterations.md`.

## I. The 3D animation

- **I1. Every piece, always.** One instance of every real part on the table from the first frame; unused cards in a visible pile; secret cards face down and flipped at the reveal; nothing hidden. Check: `qa_intersections.js` inventory on the unstarted table (test and coupon sheets excluded).
- **I2. Nothing teleports, nothing overlaps, the rules are obeyed.** Every move travels its legal route; one piece per space physically; the page asserts each move against the engine and logs ILLEGAL; anything spent flies somewhere visible. Check: `verify_anim.js` and `templates/judge-animation.md`; `qa_intersections.js` scenes 0 pairs.
- **I3. One log.** Action text appears in one fixed-height scrolling log only; captions say who does what and why; players speak in short lines. Check: judge-animation.
- **I4. Materials render true.** Procedural non-repeating grain, subtle and fine; textures bound to surfaces; occlusion correct; contact shadows. The owner's woods (2026-09-18): walnut is a grey-brown, well short of orange ("the walnut i have is more desaturated in color than the walnut you are rendering"); walnut engraving keeps the grain and reads a little darker, a little redder and glossier than the surface ("it also engraves dark instead of light ... slighty more red, a little bit darker, more specular, and does have grain"), never a flat pale or black mark; every cut edge is a charred dark brown on any wood ("cut edges should render as a burnt dark brown"); contact shadows lie on the table and are not drawn when the camera is under it ("shadows on the table shouldn't be rendered when looking at the spread from underneath"). `lib/render3d.js`: `WOODP[kind].col` and `.eng`, `EDGE`, the shadow pass gated on the camera's height. Check: page screenshots on a real GPU, one from below the table.

## J. Code and process

- **J1. Everything is JavaScript**, runs under node and in the browser from one source (the engine, the geometry, the page, the checks). No Python. Check: the folder.
- **J2. Never a fallback.** A missing library, font, WASM, file or field throws a clear error; no silent substitutes, retries or `x || default` hiding a missing input. Check: code review; grep for candidate lists and catch-and-continue.
- **J3. One renderer, one geometry library, one repository.** `render3d.js` and `lasergeom.js` are shared; edits are backward compatible with a changelog note; each session commits only its own game's paths; generated outputs are regenerated, never auto-merged. Check: git log.
- **J4. Fast self-play.** Thousands of games per second in the engine ("2000 games ought to take less than a second"). Check: `bg stats` timing.
- **J5. Regenerate everything from code** in one command; the SVGs, the 3D, the animation, the manual figures come from one geometry source; the check chain runs after every change and before every delivery. Check: `reference/checks.md`'s chain, green.
- **J6. Portable skill.** The skill needs nothing from the author's machine: the engine (`https://github.com/jschall/boardgame-engine`: the geometry bundle, the renderer, the box, the jig, the packer, the page machinery, the rulebook build, every check, and ORCHARD, a complete starter game with example type and `page.css`) is a git submodule of every game; `engine/bin/new_game.js` builds a working game anywhere. A game never edits `engine/`. Check: `new_game.js` on a fresh folder, then `bg all`.
