## The shoulder box

Two open trays drawn from **one frame** (one wall set, one floor design, each tray widened by its own ease on every side), the lid roomier than the base: the neck's band ends span the frame's real inside, the base gets 0.10 mm a side at those ends and the lid 0.50 mm (owner, 2026-09-17: a base that just held the neck was "a pretty good fit" but should be "a bit easier"; a lid riding on the neck body with 0.30 mm a side needed "a hair more clearance"). The neck is symmetric end for end (two grip bands at each end, the middle band that shows in the shadow line with its fingers behind the mating boards), so it carries no BASE mark. Floors 3 mm inside the wall bottom (tabs through the walls), walls 12.5–24 mm high, corner box joints. A **neck** in one tray: plain strips glued inside the tray 0.2 mm in from the walls (no joints or tabs, so the outside of the tray is unbroken for the cover art), or a walnut ring pressed in with a 0.1 mm grip on its lowest bands. Closed, the neck slides into the other tray and holds the rims **2.5–4 mm apart**: that recessed gap is the shadow line, and thumb notches (16 × 3) in the lid's long walls let you push the halves apart. Closed height = 2 × wall + gap. The neck stands on the floor stack of its own tray, never on the far tray's floor.

- Check that **everything fits inside closed**: the fit checker's "packed box" scene with every part in its storage place. Assembled standees must lie flat: a Ø 24 base disc could not fit a 21.5 mm interior; bar feet 23.6 × 10 did.
- Cover art on the outside of the lid, with the small-parts safety warning in its own clear band (wording, sizes and separation in `art.md`); a medallion, logo and the product code (UPC-A, 0.5 mm modules, bars along the raster axis, 4.5 mm quiet zones) under the base; the rules inside the lid; the setup map inside the base. Every outside face (lid top, base underside, the outside of each wall) = the front file, face up while the cuts run, because the face down on the honeycomb gets smoke-stained; inside faces = the backs file; check mirroring and joint handedness after moving a face.
- The lid tray doubles as the token tray during play.

## Detents, flexures (if the game has click wheels)

- Bump-and-slot detent, not a finger: 0.5 mm bump on the pocket wall, 0.8 mm curved slot 1.6 mm behind it, 36 mm fixed-ended beam (~0.45% strain). Disc Ø 43.0 in a Ø 43.2 pocket (0.2 diametral); with the disc against the far wall the bump still engages a 3.6 × 1.0 V-notch by 0.3 mm. Notches **in phase** with the bumps (the fit checker found them 30° out).
- Test strip: three pockets varying the bump height (0.4 / 0.5 / 0.6) at the shipped clearance.
- Engrave glue reliefs under flexure beams if the layer below is glued, or glue would bond the beam.

## Standees and pieces

- Kitten proportions and big eyes read from 60 cm; markings on ears and body, **never over the eyes or nose** (the owner spotted a spot over a face at once); two bare highlights per eye (evenodd holes); a collar with a bare tag; feet as a bar (23.6 × 10) so a standee lies flat 10 mm thick in the box.
- Engrave both faces of every standee (backs file), else the back is bare wood in the 3D view and on the table.
- Cats/pieces at the side of a board stand along the wall (a 41 mm body hits a wall 1.2 mm away); the base turns with the body.
- One piece per space, physically: a hex or ring base that sits in the space so two cannot share it.

## Wheels, boards, tiles

- Hex tiles 46 mm across flats, 1.5 mm rounded corners, stitched border 4.6 mm in; three standee spots per tile as slots in the quadrants.
- A frame layer (3 mm walnut with wells) loose on a 1.5 mm backing whose engraving shows through the empty wells makes a growth track with no glue.
- Faces Ø 47 at 48 mm pitch: 1 mm air between turning neighbours.
