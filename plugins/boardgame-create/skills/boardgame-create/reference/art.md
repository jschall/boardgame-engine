# Art rules for two-tone laser engraving

What the art judges and the owner kept finding. Apply before the first judge round; it halves the rounds.

## Two tones, and only two

- Engraved or bare. Solid fills read; hatching and greys do not. Detail inside a solid field is a **bare** hole (evenodd), e.g. eye highlights, a bare digit in a badge, a bare tail on a dark rump.
- A dark character is walnut with cut-through eyes, a nose hole, a smile slot and scored lines: no engraving on walnut (it barely shows). A light character is basswood with engraved features.
- No white fills, no opacities, no strokes without fills for "light lines" (a thin black stroke IS an engraved line; keep it ≥ 0.45 mm).

## Feature sizes (40 W diode, 3 mm ply)

- Strokes of about 0.45 mm and bare gaps of about 0.6 mm after the kerf shrink are what reads well; they are guidance for legibility, not a rule (the owner: "the laser doesn't really have a minimum that we have to enforce per se"). The lint warns below them; a judge should ask whether the mark reads, not measure it. Engraving is unioned and shrunk by kerf/2 in the file, and a score line follows its finished edge (except along cuts), which is what makes fine detail crisp. Nothing engraved within 0.6–0.8 mm of a cut edge unless it deliberately bleeds past the edge (kerf/2 on fronts, 0.25 mm + kerf/2 on backs); specks < 0.6 mm² dropped or grown to 0.62 mm dots (an i-dot must never vanish).
- Lettering: outlined, ≥ 2.2 mm cap height for rules text, ≥ 3 mm for labels; condensed sans for dense rules columns, serif for titles; numbers left-aligned in rules lists (centre justification misaligned every step number).
- **Set lettering, never clean it.** The gap-closing finish pass (thicken / close) that fixes drawn art bridges letters into a strike-through and fills the counters of small glyphs. Choose one stroke weight per face and size on a sample line, and skip the closing pass on text. Letter-space small text so no gap between glyphs is under 0.6 mm (for Fredoka that was 0.75 mm at 4.3 mm caps and 0.55 mm at 3.6 mm caps).
- Antennae 2 mm, wings behind the body, stripes as curved bands clipped to the body, legs filled, whole honeycomb cells only (no cut-off cells at a border), whole leaves only.

## Composition

- Faces stay on plain wood: no patch, spot or stripe over an eye or the nose. Both eyes, nose, mouth, whiskers, inner ears visible.
- Every piece must read as what it is from 60 cm: a silhouette test (fill it black, ask what it is).
- Distinct pieces are distinct at a glance: one expression and one coat treatment each; four colonies with four base shapes (circle, hexagon, square, flower).
- Text and long marks run along the sheet's long axis (raster direction). Compose cover art in a landscape frame and rotate it onto the portrait sheet.
- Box: the lid top is the hero (a medallion, a scene, a ribbon title, at most a one-line subtitle in plain words, never a phrase stack of verbs); the four lid walls and four base walls read as **one picture across the shadow line** (a sunflower on the base lines up with a bee diving down the lid wall); clouds and solid shapes closed and knocked out; framed rectangles where floor tabs show through are part of the design.
- Inside the lid: the rules as short columns along the long axis; inside the base: the setup map (hex edges shared, bold labels, player counts); under the base: a medallion and the product code.
- Modest figure sizes on the page: a piece figure 120–260 px wide; "this is ridiculously huge" was the owner's reaction to full-width figures.

## Text on wood (owner's rule, 2026-09-15: "not useless text all over the place")

Engraved text is permanent, costs raster time, competes with the art, and turns a crafted object into a manual. A word earns its place on a part only if a player will **read it during play, at that spot, and could not get it any other way**.

Text that belongs on the wood:
- The title once, on the cover, with at most a one-line subtitle; the product code under the base; the small-parts safety warning (below). Legally required text is never filler.
- Labels that are read during play and cannot be inferred: a player's name on their station, a value on a tile or card, a numbered track, the name of a pile that would otherwise be ambiguous.
- One rules reference in one place, out of the playing area (inside the lid is the natural place), set as short plain sentences in play order, never compressed into fragments (if they do not fit as sentences, cut rules from the panel). Only if the game has a turn sequence people forget.
- A per-player aid card is allowed as well, because the lid is out of reach at the table: then the two serve different moments and do not repeat each other. The aid holds the turn (the steps, in order, one line each); the lid holds setup, the end and scoring. No sentence appears in both, and neither appears on the board.

Text that does not:
- Handling and assembly instructions ("this tray is the lid: turn it over onto the box"). A player learns that once; it goes in the page's instructions.
- Rules digests and phase strips on the board or tiles ("CLICK · SNEAK · PAWS · STASH", "move up to 2 · grab · bank"). The rules live in one place; the board is a picture.
- Captions under icons that already read, names on parts whose shape identifies them, taglines, mottos, slogans, decorative words, explanations of what a thing is, anything a developer wrote for themselves.

The rule is about engraving. Page prose is judged separately: the page keeps its tagline and the principles-check section the owner asked for; what it must not do is repeat the rules three times.

Two tests. **The manual test:** if the sentence could appear in a rulebook, it belongs in the page or inside the lid, never on a playing surface. **The 60 cm test:** from 60 cm a part should read as a picture; if a word cannot be read from there it is not helping anyone during play, and if it can, ask who needs it at that moment. Prefer an icon to a word, and a word to a sentence. Where text stays, set it properly: outlined, along the raster axis, at least 2.2 mm cap height, and never in two places.

## The small-parts safety warning (owner's request, 2026-09-15)

Every game has small parts, so the box carries the choking-hazard warning in the form 16 CFR 1500.19 prescribes, even though that section only *mandates* it for toys and games intended for children aged 3 to under 6 (our games are for adults; carrying it anyway is what retailers expect and what the owner asked for). Verified against the regulation on 2026-09-15:

- **Wording and symbol:** the safety-alert triangle (an exclamation mark in a triangle), then `WARNING: CHOKING HAZARD—Small parts. Not for children under 3 yrs.` The signal word and the hazard statement are in capitals; keep the wording exact.
- **Where:** on the principal display panel, which for a shoulder box is the lid top, and repeated in the page's components section and any printed manual.
- **Type size** (16 CFR 1500.121 Table 1, capital-letter height, by principal display panel area): a lid over 30 sq in (about 194 cm²; a 190 mm square lid is 56 sq in) needs the signal word at 5/32 in (4.0 mm), the statement of hazard at 7/64 in (2.8 mm) and other cautionary text at 3/32 in (2.4 mm). "CHOKING HAZARD" and "Small parts" are the hazard statement, so set them at 2.8 mm or more, not the 2.4 mm of the rest. Smaller lids: 15–30 sq in needs 1/8, 3/32 and 5/64 in; 10–15 sq in needs 7/64, 3/32 and 1/16 in.
- **The symbol:** a bare exclamation mark knocked out of a solid triangle reads as an "A" at 5 mm. What works: a 7.5 mm triangle drawn as a 0.7 mm outline with an engraved bar and dot inside, each kept 0.6 mm from the outline.
- **Layout:** two-tone engraved, in sharp contrast (engraved on bare wood qualifies), separated on all sides from other printed or graphic matter by a border line or by a clear space at least as tall as the smallest cautionary type; give it its own band inside the cover frame and recentre the composition above it.
- The geometry library should provide it as one call (`choking_warning(panel_w, panel_h, t)` with the sizes chosen from the panel area) so no game re-derives the sizes.

## Generated, never hand-drawn

Every figure on the page comes from the real cut files. Stale hand-drawn diagrams were replaced on request. The rulebook carries the figures (captured from the cut files by the engine's `manual/assets.js`); the page has no art section.

## What judges found, round after round (check these first)

Clipped outlines · engraving colliding with cuts or holes · flowers that do not read as their species · labels crowding the tile edge · stripes as straight bars instead of curved bands · antennae too short · a skep that is a dome not a bell · a door on the centre line · acorns too small · well outlines under a frame (double lines) · small labels under icons (unreadable) · text overflowing a card border · a badge that clashes with the token symbol · dashes that do not centre on corners · uneven wrap on card text · a knob too thin to hold · a queen's crown drawn but not cut · a wasp with stingers too sharp to survive · empty quadrants · a tree with no trunk · ground bands running into the wall's end joint.
