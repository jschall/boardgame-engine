# The deliverable: one self-contained HTML page

Generated by the engine's `page/build.js` (`node engine/bin/bg.js page`) from the game's `table.js` and `page.js`; the machinery is the engine's `page/page.js` (ORCHARD's `table.js` and BUMBLE's are the worked examples of the GameTable contract in `engine/README.md`). Everything embedded: the part SVGs (as JSON), the sheets (each once, as plain text in a `<script type="text/plain" class="sheet-svg">` block after page.js; page.js gives the cards blob URLs), the rules engine, the renderer, the showcase game, the print rulebook. Open from `file://` with no network. Sections, in this order, with these ids (the shape the owner settled on 2026-09-17; the art section is optional now that the rulebook carries the figures):

0. No header at all. The title, one plain line and "2 to 4 players · 30 to 40 minutes" sit over the 3D stage while the boxes are closed and fade as the box opens. The owner, on a three-sentence lede: "no one will read this, not even me." Nothing on the page that nobody reads.

1. `#demo` **The 3D stage is the whole page.** One full-viewport canvas (`100vh`, full browser width); nothing scrolls (`html, body { overflow:hidden }`). **The opening** plays when the reader presses the one button on the stage, "Open the box" (BUMBLE: 10 s, most of it for the pieces): two closed boxes stand on an empty stage (one showing its lid, one its underside), the second dissolves, the table fades in, the first lies down, its lid flies to its place on the table and every packed piece rises out of the base and flies to where the table keeps it. Every frame is a pure function of one progress value, so it runs backwards; the button reads "Close the box" while the box opens or the game runs and runs the opening backwards (from the live table when a game is on: the pieces snap to the engine's positions, the opening is rebuilt from that table, and opening it again lets the game go on); a loading screen covers the stage until the first frame, and the flight plan is computed between frames after it (the opening waits at the edge of the flights, loading screen back, if it gets there first); `prefers-reduced-motion` shows the assembled table and the game. Then the game, which **starts by itself when the box is open and plays game after game; there are no controls** (the owner: "there should probably be no controls at all for the gameplay"). The score line and **one fixed-height log** are transparent text over the view (the log takes no pointer). Three buttons at the top right open the rulebook modal (the book alone: a click or a swipe turns the page, the leaf bends and throws a shadow, no toolbar, no text), the parts panel (the scene in parts mode, with the list, Explode and the descriptions) and the laser-files modal (which also carries the two rulebook PDF links); the game pauses behind them. Free orbit (drag), pan (right-drag), zoom (the wheel, which is also a trackpad pinch) from the closed boxes through the opening and the game. Home views are shifted clear of the parts panel, and up on phones so the table clears the log.
2. `#rules`, in `#modal-rules` **The rulebook.** The real print rulebook rendered client-side as a page-turning book (BUMBLE's `manual/` generator and `manual-embed.js` / `manual-viewer.js`), with the PDF links in this one place and nowhere else. No Read-larger mode (page turning broke in it). Rules text must match the engine exactly (the same constants, printed from the engine's `RULES`). It is an end-user document: nothing about cutting, production or assembly is printed in it.
3. `#art` **The art** (optional). 2D figures generated from the real part SVGs (cuts as thin dark lines, engraving in burn colour on a wood swatch), grouped, each figure 120–260 px wide. Nothing hand-drawn, nothing stale. BUMBLE dropped it once the rulebook carried the figures.
4. `#files`, in `#modal-files` **The SVG files.** One card per sheet (backs sheets and the test sheet included) with a preview image and a download link; the colour legend (red cut; blue score, run after engraving and before cutting at low power; black engrave; magenta corner marks on backs files; kerf compensated in the file, machine compensation off); the order of work (kerf coupon and joint samples first, backs before fronts, the registration flip); the stock inputs with the kerf formula. Nothing else on the page.

## What is on the page, and what is not

On the page: the stage (the opening and the game, standard G2); the three nav buttons (Rulebook, Parts, Laser files) and their modals (G3); the title, one plain line and the player-count pill over the closed boxes; the box button; the score line and the transparent log during the game; in the files modal the production sheets in rows (name, front, back, description), the test sheets as cards, the colour legend, the order of work, the stock inputs with the kerf formula, and the list of bought components; in the parts modal every part and assembly with Explode, the jig assembly and the packed box.

Not on the page: a header or hero copy, a lede, a tagline, "about" or "why" prose, a second copy of the rules (the book is the rules), the components table repeated from the book, the fit-tolerance boilerplate ("Fits drawn for your stock's thinnest and thickest readings…" was removed on request), the design principles check, stale sections from brainstorming, self-play tables presented as fun, production or assembly instructions beyond the order of work, and controls for the game (it plays itself). If the owner would not read it, it is not there.

## The page has a custom style

The engine does not pick a typeface, a palette, or a loading animation. Those are the game's, and a game that still looks like ORCHARD has not done this step.

- **`fonts/`** and **`game.json.fonts`** `{ family, R, SB, B }` (filenames; `family` is the CSS name). Three weights, a free licence file beside them. They are the engraved letters, the page title, and the rulebook. A missing file throws.
- **`page.css`** is required. It sets `--ink --soft --cream --paper --accent --accent-d --ok --line --display --body` and the look of `#loading` (the engine only positions the overlay; the game paints it: colour, type, the animation, the word). The title, the box button, the nav, the log and the modals follow those tokens. ORCHARD's `page.css` is one example.
- **`manual/manual.css`** is the book's face (the same family). Do not ship the starter's cream-and-honey sheet unchanged if this game is not that look.
- The loading screen is part of the design, not a leftover: it is on from the first byte until the first frame (G7), and it should belong to this game.

## How the instructions read (owner's rule, 2026-09-15)

The owner's standard: "plain sentences instead of slogan fragments", and a review of the manual and all instruction text "for ai-slop language patterns" to remove them. It covers every place rules appear: the page's rules and questions, the printed manual, the rules inside the lid, player aids and card text.

1. Complete, plain sentences, in the order a player needs them. Not "NEST wasp to a flower within 2; its bees drop nectar, go home." but "When you plant a flower with a wasp nest, move the wasp to any flower within two steps of it. Every bee on that flower drops its nectar and flies home." No slogans or phrase stacks ("plant · fly · dance"), no header-colon shorthand in place of a sentence.
2. None of the AI patterns: hype adjectives; "not just X, it's Y"; rhetorical questions; dash asides; triplets for rhythm; words like delve, journey, elevate, seamless, vibrant, unleash; closing summaries that restate what was just said.
3. When a wood panel (the lid inside, an aid card) is too small for sentences, cut rules from the panel and leave them to the manual or the page. Never compress them into fragments to make them fit.
4. The test: does it read like a patient friend explaining the game at the table? Numbers where a player needs them, one idea per sentence, defined terms used consistently.
5. Everyday words only. The owner: "definitely don't use the word skep in the instructions. no one will know what it means." No specialist word a family would not know, in manuals, page rules, box text or piece names: "beehive" for skep, "nectar" not "forage", "token" not "meeple" unless the game defines it. Internal part ids in code may keep the technical name; the player never sees those.
6. The rules judge and the page judge mark fragments, these patterns and jargon words as flaws.

## Every piece on the table, always (owner's rule, 2026-09-15)

"the table view should always show *every* piece at all times. if there are cards not in play, they should be in a stack somewhere on the table or in the box." This came up when a page gave every player the same wish card because all card instances shared one part and the other eleven appeared nowhere.

- Every physical piece is an instance of its own real part, placed from the first frame: the unstarted table shows the complete inventory (every tile, token, card, standee, the box).
- Secret cards lie face down (`flipped`) by their owner and flip at the reveal; never swap a part in to "become" the card.
- Cards and pieces not in play sit in a visible face-down pile on the table or in the box tray.
- No instance in the table scene is ever `hidden`.
- `qa_intersections.js` with a parts file checks this: it counts visible instances per part id in the unstarted table and compares them with the copies on the game sheets.

## Animation rules (the owner watches closely)

- The opening (above) shows the box, the lid coming off and every piece flying to its place when the reader presses the button; the game then plays from the engine's event log on that same table and ends with the scoring shown.
- **Nothing teleports.** Every move travels visibly along its legal route, including moves off the board and back on, pieces bumped or swapped, pieces returning to the supply. A lifted disc rises above a standee's head before the standee moves under it, so nothing clips through anything.
- **Nothing overlaps.** Not in the simulation, not in the animation: one piece per space, physically.
- **The animation follows the rules exactly.** The page asserts each hop against the engine's state and logs `ILLEGAL` and stops if they disagree; a special move through a normally shut route is marked on the board (a coloured marker and a caption naming the rule) so a viewer does not read it as a bug.
- Anything eaten, spent or discarded flies somewhere visible (a discard stack, the box), never fades out.
- Captions say who does what and why ("Ada clicks the wheel to open the way to the vault"); players speak in short lines at the right moments; round headers in the log.

## Live stock (G5)

The files modal takes two caliper readings and a kerf per stock (`#t3lo=2.67&t3hi=2.92&kerf_t3=0.18`, keys from the game's stocks). A worker in the page regenerates the parts, the 3D view and the SVG downloads from the same generator `bg parts` runs. Defaults come from `game.json` / the built `META.params`. The typefaces in that generator are the game's `fonts/` (`game.json.fonts`), not a face the engine picked.

## Test hooks the QA scripts rely on (keep them)

| hook | purpose |
|---|---|
| `window.__intro.ready` | true once the opening's flight plan is made (or there is no opening); `__signalReady` fires at the first frame after that; harnesses that drive `__intro.set(p)` wait for it |
| `#shot=table\|box\|parts&id=<pid or asm>&p=<pitch>&y=<yaw>&v=<view>&cx=&cy=&cz=` | open a tab and set the camera (used by `shot.js`, `render_for_judge.js`, `qa_intersections.js`); any `#shot=` skips the opening, so the unstarted-table scene in `qa_scenes.json` must load `#shot=table` |
| `#intro=<p>` | freeze the opening at progress p (0 to 1) for screenshots (the button hides) |
| `window.__intro.openBox()` / `closeBox()` / `rate` | the box button's two actions, and a pace multiplier for the harnesses (`rate = 4` opens in a quarter of the time) |
| `window.__intro` | `{p, target, live, end, pieces, set(p), finish()}`: the opening's state; `finish()` restores the table pose; `window.__introDebug` summarises the flight plan |
| `window.__qa().playing` | the demo is running (it starts by itself when the box is open) |
| `window.__modal.open(name)` | 'rules', 'parts' or 'files', as the nav buttons do |
| `#shot=box&lift=1` | the lid lifted |
| `#shot=table&anim=N&speed=S` | start the animation at speed S and pause after N turns |
| `window.__scene` | the live scene (`static`, `dynamic`, `basis`, `world`) for the in-page intersection check |
| `window.__qa()` | `{turn, over, illegal}` |
| `window.__maxSnap` | largest jump any piece would make when re-synced from the engine after a turn; the teleport metric, must stay ≈ 0 |
| `window.__signalReady` | called (if defined) after the first drawn frame and any test action; `shot.js` injects it and waits |
| `window.__api` | `part, mk, scene, log, tween, flyTo, alongPath …` for ad-hoc tests |

Headless notes: on the owner's machine headless Chromium reaches the real GPU with `--use-angle=default --enable-gpu --ignore-gpu-blocklist` (`BG_GPU=1` for any engine check): prefer it for anything judged by eye, and watch the opening by pressing the button (`engine/checks/scroll_sheet.js` is a contact sheet of that, not a window scroll). Never only freeze frames with `#intro=`. Under swiftshader a capture re-rasterizes big blurred layers (a title text-shadow, a vignette) and can take a minute per frame, and any page with a looping CSS animation needs `animations: 'disabled'` in the screenshot call. Otherwise playwright chromium with `--use-angle=swiftshader --enable-unsafe-swiftshader` for WebGL; a full game page is several megabytes and can take over 30 s to reach its load event on a busy machine, so the browser scripts allow 180 s for navigation (`NAV_TIMEOUT_MS`); the in-page check's animated scenes play forward in real time, so a full scene set on a big game takes about 15 minutes; screenshots through `shot.js` (defines `__signalReady`, waits for the first frame, captures the viewport or one element such as `.cwrap` or `#files` at its true height; Chrome's own `--screenshot` with `scrollIntoView` produced blank captures); **animation tests in real time** (`verify_anim.js`), never with Chrome's `--virtual-time-budget` (it stalls requestAnimationFrame tweens after the first one, which looks like a bug and is not). Capture sections by selector, not with a tall window. The page script is a real file (`page.js`) inlined by `build.js`, so ordinary comments are fine in it.

## Layout details that were requested

The stage uses the full browser width; figures at modest size; no repeating CSS gradients as the page background; the log fixed-height; the parts viewer on a light plain background; the closed-box home camera low enough to show the shadow line.
