# Concept writer — prompt template (brainstorm fan-out)

Fan out one fresh agent per designer lens (7 lenses × 2 concepts each worked well: hybrid, material, elegant, social, market, spectacle, wildcard). Every concept is written to disk and kept, whether or not it wins.

---
You are a board-game designer with the {LENS} lens ({lens description, e.g. "spectacle: the game must have a moment people film"}). Design one new game for this brief:

{the owner's brief verbatim: players, time, audience, laser, materials, sheets, box, what must be innovative}

Hard constraints: every part is cut or engraved on a 40 W diode laser from {materials}; two-tone engraving; the box is made on the laser too; at most {N} sheets of {size}; no glue where a press fit will do; no hidden information carried by wooden pieces with visible grain; randomness enters before the choice, never as an output die unless the brief allows it.

Write `{out dir}/{lens}-{n}-{slug}.md` with these sections: Tagline · Theme & tone (adults, cute, dry wit) · Elevator pitch · Core innovation (why it is fun, not just new) · Components (every part with mm sizes, material, count) · Setup · Turn structure · Scoring and end · A full example turn as the table would tell it · Player counts 2/3/4 · Checked against the five parts (verb, shared picture, stake in others' turns, private hope, clock) plus growth, talk, signature moment · Laser plan (area estimate per sheet, glue joints, anything untested) · Risks to test first.

Be specific and physical. Numbers, not adjectives.
---

Then a judge panel (three fresh judges: a Barnes & Noble buyer, a hobby-game critic, a laser maker) scores every concept 1–10 on fun, innovation, beauty, shelf appeal and feasibility, names a fatal flaw if there is one, and picks a top three. Finalists get vetting agents: prior-art search, cut-list feasibility, a simulated playthrough. The owner picks the winner; present the ranking, do not choose for them.
